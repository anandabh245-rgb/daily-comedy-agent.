3. Title: Why I Started Pretending To Read Dostoyevsky At The Gym

***

4. Spoken Monologue:

My entire personality is currently hanging on by a thread because I decided I needed to look chic reading heavy literature in public. 

So I bought a five-hundred-page translation of Dostoyevsky. Not because I want to understand nineteenth-century Russian existentialism, but because I need strangers on the subway to think I have a deeply troubled inner life. 

The problem is, I actually brought it to the gym. I am sitting on the stationary bike, sweating through a polyester shirt, frantically turning pages of *Crime and Punishment* while my heart rate hits one-seventy. 

People are staring at me. Not because I’m working out, but because I look like I’m about to commit a felony over a stolen loaf of bread while doing cardio. 

Yesterday, a guy walked up to me and asked if I was okay. I wasn't. Raskolnikov had just pawned an axe, and my left thigh was cramping so hard I almost transcended reality. 

I’m not getting smarter; I’m just getting exhausted while looking like a pretentious Victorian ghost. Next week, I’m just going to hold a sketchbook and stare blankly into the middle distance like everyone else. At least charcoal pencils don't give you paper cuts when you drop them mid-squat.

And the worst part? He didn't even ghost me. He replied twelve hours later with a meme of a cat staring blankly at a wall. 

Which, honestly? Fair. That cat understands me better than Sarah’s whiteboard does. At least the cat isn’t trying to diagnose his attachment style using a TikTok quiz made by a fourteen-year-old.

4. Spoken Monologue:

By midnight, my workspace looked like an Apple flagship store inside a monk's cell, and I was staring at a blank document questioning my entire career path. 

It turns out that cleaning your room isn’t self-care; it’s just procrastination wearing a very expensive linen button-down.
